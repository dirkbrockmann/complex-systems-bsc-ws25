## The Gray-Scott Model

In the [second lecture](/lectures/lecture-7) on pattern formation we discussed the Turing Mechanism and gave a few
examples of reaction diffusion systems that generated interesting spatio-temporal patterns.
In [the last slide](/lectures/lecture-7/slides/slide-14-gray-scott) I showed you the famous Gray-Scott model, defined by these equations for
the two chemical species $U$ and $V$:
$$
\partial_t u =  -uv^2 + F(1-u) + D_u \partial_x^2 u
$$
$$
\partial_t v =  uv^2 -(F+k)v + D_v \partial_x^2 v
$$

The reaction part was derived from the basic reactions of the Gray-Scott model:

$$
U+2V\rightarrow 3V \qquad V\rightarrow \emptyset.
$$

We discussed that this is similar to a simple infection process, the only difference is that
in the Gray-Scott model **two** "infected" individuals $V$ infect a susceptible individual $U$.
