## Assignment 2: The logistic map, period doubling and chaos

In the [second lecture](/lectures/lecture-2) we introduced the logistic map:

$$
x_{n+1}=\lambda(1-x_{n})x_{n}\qquad x_n\in [0,1], \lambda \in [0,4]
$$

based on a simple, discrete time model for reproduction, growth, and dynamics 
of a population of a species in an ecological context.
We discussed how the behavior of the model depends on the key growth 
parameter $\lambda$, how fixpoints can be computed, how they
loose stability, that period doubling occurs and, finally, 
when a critical $\lambda\approx 3.569946...$ is reached the dynamics becomes
aperiodic and chaotic and therefore appear to be unpredicatable. We also
discussed that this implies sensitivity to initial conditions.

## A: The Bifurcation diagram

Revisit the [bifurcation diagram](/lectures/lecture-2/slides/slide-4-bifurcationdiagram/) we discussed in class. In the bifurcation diagram,
you can see the period doubling events at the sequence of critical values of the growth
parameter $\lambda$. We discussed in class that the distance ($\lambda_n-\lambda_{n-1}$) between successive
period doubling events becomes smaller and smaller so that eventually, at some point
the period is infinite, after an infinite amount of period doubling events. 
Beyond that point ($\lambda\approx 3.569946...$) all sequences of the map are aperiodic.

1. Using the interactive bifurcation diagram, identify period doubling
   bifurcation points $\lambda_1$, $\lambda_2$, $\lambda_3$ and $\lambda_4, ...$ and make a table of them.

2. Compute the ratios:

$$
\delta_n = \frac{\lambda_{n} - \lambda_{n-1}}{\lambda_{n+1} - \lambda_{n}}
$$

This ratios is named the **Feigenbaum constant** (see [Lecture Notes on the Logistic Map](/lectures/lecture-2)).

3. Inspect the bifurcation diagram closely. Way beyond the critical $\lambda\approx 3.569946...$, 
in the regime of aperiodic dynamics, you can see bands and gaps of less complexity. 
What is going on there?
Explain what the behavior looks like around the value $\lambda\approx 3.83$. 
You can use 
[the sequence generator](/lectures/lecture-2/slides/slide-2-time-series/) and
the [Cobwebbing Tool](/lectures/lecture-2/slides/slide-3-cobwebbing/) 
to investigate what is going on. Explain what the logistic map behaves like here, and why it
is puzzling.


## Another chaotic map

Another system with similar behavior is the map defined by:

$$
x_{n+1}=\lambda (1-2x_{n}^2).
$$

This system is defined for $x\in [-1,1]$ and for $\lambda \in [0,1]$.

1. Compute the fixpoints of this system.
