in statistical physics and complex systems. Here's the Complexity Explorable
**Barista's Secret**, which illustrates the model:

The idea is simple: Imagine water flowing through a porous medium like coffee grounds. 
Whether the water can percolate through depends on how tightly the medium is packed. 


## States and Setup


This is a lattice model, so you'll work with patches again. Make the world 64x64 pixels.

Each patch can be in one of three states:
- **coffee** (brown)
- **empty** (white)
- **water** (blue)

Define a patch-specific variable `state` and write a `paint` function that colors patches according to their state.

In the `setup` function:
1. Initialize patches randomly as either "empty" or "coffee". The fraction of coffee patches should be controlled by a slider (range 0 to 1).
2. Create a small disk of "water" patches in the center of the world. You can filter patches by their coordinates (`pxcor`/`pycor`) or use `distancexy 0 0` to get the distance from the origin.

## Simulation

The dynamics are straightforward: at each tick, every water patch turns its neighboring 
**empty** patches into water.

You can structure this with:
```
ask patches with [state = "water"] [
  ask neighbors with [state = "empty"] [
    ...
  ]
]
```

## Observations

Run simulations with different coffee fractions. Try to find the critical concentration just sufficient for the water to reach the boundaries of the system.
